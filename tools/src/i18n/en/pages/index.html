Why we exist

When mothers go to prison, children lose their home.

In Kenya, mothers are often imprisoned for poverty-related offences. Their children are left without care – many would end up on the street.

The Nest takes in exactly these children, as well as abandoned babies. It provides care, food and education and reunites mothers with their children after release. The goal: bringing families back into society on stable footing.

We, the association Watoto wa Nest e.V., are a young volunteer team – many of us worked at The Nest as volunteers ourselves. We raise funds in Germany and make sure they reach The Nest directly.

The project

Three houses, one goal: protect children and reunite families.

The Nest was founded in the early 1990s as a prevention and reintegration programme. Today it consists of three facilities in and around Limuru, 20 km north-west of Nairobi.

The Nest Children's Home

Since 1997, up to 100 children aged 3 to 18 have been cared for, schooled and given medical treatment here.

Nest Baby Village

The baby home – opened in March 2014 – cares for abandoned babies and toddlers around the clock.

Halfway House for mothers

Here, released mothers are reunited with their children and supported by social workers on their way back into society.
